FireWise Education

What Is FireWise?

FireWise is a nationally recognized NFPA program that teaches landowners how to reduce their wildfire risk through practical preparation. Cedar Ridge LLC is now offering FireWise services — because our crews have seen firsthand what makes properties survive and what makes them burn. This knowledge belongs in the community.

The Three Defensible Space Zones

Defensible space is the buffer between your home and surrounding vegetation. Organized into three zones, it's the single most effective thing a homeowner can do to improve wildfire survivability.

01
0 – 5 Feet
Immediate Zone

The area directly against your home — the most critical. This is where embers land first. Make it as non-combustible as possible.

  • Non-combustible decking, fencing, and siding materials
  • Remove all dead plants, leaves, and pine needles
  • Clean gutters and roof surfaces of debris
  • Replace wood mulch with gravel or rock
  • No combustible storage under decks or porches
02
5 – 30 Feet
Intermediate Zone

A reduced-fuel buffer that slows fire and prevents it from "laddering" into tree canopies toward your home. Critical for survivability.

  • Islands of plants separated by non-combustible spacing
  • Mow grass to a maximum of 4 inches tall
  • Remove lower tree limbs up to 6–10 feet off the ground
  • Prune shrubs and maintain proper plant spacing
  • Remove all dead vegetation consistently
03
30 – 100 Feet
Outer Zone

Reduce the fuel load at a wider scale so any approaching fire has less intensity and momentum when it reaches your property.

  • Remove dead trees, snags, and wood debris
  • Space out trees and tall shrubs to reduce spread
  • Keep grass short during dry and fire seasons
  • Remove highly flammable plants (juniper, rosemary, etc.)
  • Coordinate with neighbors to extend mitigation

🔥 Your FireWise Action Plan — Start Here

1
Assess Your Risk

Walk your property and identify fuel sources — dead vegetation, wood piles, dense shrubs near structures, and combustible materials on decks and roofs.

2
Create Defensible Space

Work through all three zones starting with the immediate zone (0–5 ft). Clear debris and thin vegetation working outward from the structure.

3
Harden Your Home

Seal gaps and vents with ember-resistant mesh, replace combustible siding and decking where possible, and install multi-pane windows to resist radiant heat.

4
Prepare to Evacuate

Have a go-bag ready, know your evacuation routes, sign up for local emergency alerts, and have a family communication plan before fire season starts.

5
Maintain Year-Round

FireWise prep isn't a one-time project. Schedule seasonal maintenance — especially before dry seasons — to keep your defensible space effective.

6
Engage Your Neighbors

Fire doesn't stop at property lines. Encourage neighbors to prepare, and consider joining or starting a community FireWise program in your area.
